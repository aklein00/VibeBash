# What he does, and how to set up a business like it

The sample vault next to this file (`vault/products/Fieldnote/`) is fiction, so the links in the example resolve. It is not Carson James's business, and it is not a product in this repo. VibeBash is a public repository. Put a real offer, a real customer note, or a real revenue export in a private vault. The risks of keeping the live memory in Obsidian, in Cursor, or in Codex are in `implementation-plan.md`.

## The work

The man on camera is Carson James. The vault on screen is his. He calls Carson James one of his businesses, "number two in the vault," and Buckaroo Crew the main product under it.

Buckaroo Crew is his online horsemanship membership: video courses, a searchable library, downloadable guides, a members' community, and Carson AI inside the member app, answering from his own teaching. There is a member portal. Public pricing on his site at the time of this note is about $19.99 a month or $199.99 a year, with a free trial.

The business is a teacher with a method, selling ongoing access to that method. The software (the app, the library, Carson AI) is how the membership is delivered. The Claude agent is the staff that runs the marketing. Those are different jobs, and the video is about the staff.

The jobs he shows:

- **Creating a campaign.** The offer, the sales page, the order forms.
- **Drafting emails.**
- **Facebook ads.**

The campaign note tells the agent to read the customer, the competitive set, and the campaign topic before it writes the hook. The product note also records where the code lives, so the same agent can be pointed at the product and not only at the copy.

This is not a trading business, a generic AI agency, or a prompt course. The daily note open at the end of the video is the session log he describes. It is not the product.

## The money, as he shows it

He says he makes about $100,000 a month, "all with AI," and cuts to a dashboard titled "The Numbers," updated Saturday, June 13, 2026. Read off that frame at 360p, so treat the dollars as approximate:

- Lead-magnet line: about $50,852 a month, 2,561 paying members, 8.9% 30-day churn, CAC about $56, working LTV about $205.
- Web and apps line: about $44,551 a month, 6,185 paying members, 7.9% churn, CAC about $26, working LTV about $91.
- LLC total recurring: about $95,403 a month, about $1.14M a year.

That is a low-priced membership, two ways in (a lead magnet, and web or apps going straight to a trial), and thousands of paying members. "All with AI" means the agent runs the campaigns, the email, and the ads for a product people already pay for. The horsemanship is what they pay for. At about $20 a month, 2,561 members on the lead-magnet line land near $50k. The claim and the public price can sit together.

Two things on that dashboard matter if you copy the shape:

- Churn near 8% a month is a leak. The campaign, email, and ad jobs carry the business, because a large slice of members has to be replaced every month. A vault does not fix that. An offer people keep using does.
- Lifetime value of about $205 and $91 against acquisition cost of about $56 and $26 is a payback of a few months, on the "conservative" label he shows. Paying members, monthly recurring revenue, churn, acquisition cost, and lifetime value are the scoreboard. He keeps them where the agent can read them. You should too, from your own billing data.

## How to set one up

**Start from a method you can teach, not from the agent.** He already had the horsemanship, the clinics, the videos, and an audience. The vault was added so an agent could run the repeating work. If you do not yet have a thing people will pay for every month, the notes will be a well-organized notebook, which is the failure he opens the video by describing.

**Sell access, not a one-off.** The shape is a membership: a library that grows, a community, a low monthly price, a short trial, and an easy cancel. His public offer is built that way. One flagship product is enough. He has more than one business in the vault and still treats Buckaroo Crew as the main product.

**Make the software a feature of the membership.** A searchable library, a member app, and an assistant trained on your own material (the way Carson AI is trained on his clinics and emails) give people a reason to stay. That assistant is part of the product. The Claude agent that writes your sales pages is the staff. Do not confuse them.

**Write the master note before you automate anything.** One folder, one note with the same name, linked to: who it is for, what is in the library, the price and the trial, where the code lives, and the five scoreboard numbers. His agent becomes useful when reading that one note pulls in the rest of the folder.

**Turn the repeating work into short job notes.** The first three are the ones he shows:

1. Create a campaign: offer, sales page, order form. The agent reads the customer and the product note before it writes.
2. Draft the emails that belong to that campaign.
3. Make the ads. A person still approves claims.

Add a fourth job for whatever you ship into the library each month, and a fifth that writes this week's numbers into the product note from the billing export. The agent should read those numbers before it writes the next campaign.

**Put the vault in git.** He syncs it so the memories cannot vanish and so a second session can see the log. The layout is in `implementation-plan.md`. Obsidian and Claude Code hold the notes. Cursor does the code you want to watch. Codex runs a job that is already specified.

**Configure keys once, outside the notes.** API credentials and MCP servers are the last thing he mentions. They are how the agent reaches mail, ads, and the models. They do not belong in the vault.

**You still choose the offer.** He does not write the daily notes. He does say which offer is going out. "We're going to do this offer" is the sentence the agent cannot invent for you. An agent filling in a campaign for an offer you have not chosen is how the vault gets confident and wrong.

## A usable first version

- **Who pays:** people who already want the skill you teach and would rather pay a small amount every month than buy a single course and stall.
- **What they get:** the library, one new piece of it on a schedule, a place to ask questions, and an assistant that answers from your material.
- **What the agent does:** drafts the sales page, the emails, and the ad brief from the job notes, and logs the day.
- **What you do:** pick the month's offer, make the one piece of teaching only you can make, approve the page, and read the five numbers before the next campaign.

Price it so a few thousand members would matter, and so someone can cancel without writing you an essay. Then the vault has something to sell.
